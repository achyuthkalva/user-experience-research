# 01 — Plan research around the decision

**Source basis:** User eXperience Research Playbook v1.2, Parts 1–6, §2.4, §3.1.1, §4.2, Appendix A. This module operationalises the source; its additional safeguards are identified in 10.

## Objective: establish what evidence must enable

Input: the actual decision, context, existing brief, known uncertainties, and available research. Output: a specific goal, research questions with stable RQ IDs, tiers, and required evidence. Exit: every question connects to a decision and its evidence need is explicit.

State: “We need to decide [decision] for [population/context] by [decision date, if known]. Research will establish [specific uncertainty].” When a deadline or decision owner is unknown, label it unknown rather than filling it with a generic guess.

Separate stakeholder beliefs, product goals, research questions, and interview questions. An interview question is an instrument; it is not automatically the study's research question.

Classify questions by both dimensions: attitudinal/behavioural and qualitative/quantitative. The dimensions describe evidence sought, not permanent labels for methods. A self-report about an action is reported behaviour, not direct observation.

### Protect essential questions and justify scope cuts

| Tier | Treatment | Required record |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tier 1 | Essential to the decision; cannot silently remove | Evidence route or explicit decision blocker; request more access, narrower decision, different timing, or explicit risk ownership |
| Tier 2 | Important; explore feasible alternatives before dropping | Alternatives considered, why insufficient, remaining impact, next evidence need |
| Tier 3 | Low impact on this decision; may defer | Acknowledge the omission and reason |

“Must answer” is a prioritisation requirement, not a guarantee the world supplies an answer. Document realistic alternatives; do not claim proof that every conceivable method has been exhausted.

### Use the evidence ladder as claim categories

| Source category | Appropriate claim when justified | Boundary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Directional signal | A problem or opportunity may exist | Does not establish prevalence or causality |
| Patterned explanation | A recurring pattern has a supported interpretation | Alternative explanations may remain |
| Sized problem | Frequency or scale is estimated for a defined population/unit | Requires sound sample, measurement, denominator, and uncertainty |
| Comparative confidence | Options differ on a defined outcome under stated conditions | Requires an appropriate comparison design |
| Business-case evidence | Operational or financial implications are quantified | Depends on measured inputs and explicit assumptions |

Preserve these five source categories, but do not treat them as an automatic scientific ranking. A financial calculation can be uncertain; more observations do not repair a biased measurement.

## Methodology: use existing evidence and feasible methods

Input: tiered questions, required claims, constraints, risks. Output: question-to-method map and constraint disclosure. Exit: a reviewer can tell what each method can and cannot answer.

First inventory recordings, previous studies, programme/product materials, analytics, support records, and relevant external evidence that are actually available. Use 04 to judge relevance, recency, independence, and coverage. Do not commission new primary research to repeat a sufficiently answered question.

For each unresolved RQ, compare candidate methods in 02 against: evidence type, audience access, data access, infrastructure, time, budget, decision risk, and limitations. Select one method or a justified sequence. Add another study only to answer a gap the first approach cannot address.

Use qualitative → quantitative when the remaining need is scale; quantitative → qualitative when the remaining need is interpretation. These are conditional routes, not a mandatory two-study recipe. Do not assume a survey creates statistical confidence simply because it has numbers.

**Constraint disclosure — all eight fields:** learning need; chosen method; why it fits constraints; supported claims; unsupported claims; known limitations; evidence category and uncertainty; follow-up that would strengthen or challenge the result.

## Scope: make boundaries explicit

Input: prioritised questions and methods. Output: in/out boundaries and scope-cut rationale. Exit: critical omissions are visible.

Specify relevant populations, segments, tasks/journeys, artifacts, sources, periods, contexts, and decisions. Avoid screen-based scope unless the study is actually about screens. Compare each proposed cut against tier, feasible method alternatives, and consequence of leaving it unanswered.

If the scope or decision changes, version the plan and reassess method fit. Do not retroactively present newly selected questions as the original approved plan.

## Essentials: check readiness before execution

Input: method, scope, and evidence requirement. Output: operating plan and readiness record. Exit: material access and permission blockers are resolved, or execution is explicitly not ready.

Specify only relevant roles: researcher/moderator, observer, recruiter, data analyst, domain/cultural reviewer, decision owner. Assign named people only when provided. A product stakeholder can champion findings, but their presence may also alter participant responses; plan accordingly.

Check the actual product/prototype/dataset, expected tasks/events, edge cases, sample access, consent and data handling, recording arrangements, accessible participation, tools, incentives, recruitment lead time, session buffers, analysis time, and deliverables.

Estimate time and cost from this context. Do not copy the source's illustrative session durations, incentives, panel turnaround, or 2–4 week calendar as promises. Record assumptions and approval status for commitments.

Agree the deliverable before collecting data: findings-only brief, report with recommendations, comparison, or another requested artifact. Preserve the right to report insufficient evidence.

## Protocol: build the appropriate collection instrument

Input: method, questions, scope, logistics. Output: instrument plus sampling/source rules and analysis intentions. Exit: neutral, realistic, traceable, reviewed, and piloted where practical.

Use 03 to build interview/usability guides, screeners, surveys, analytics definitions, experiments, or secondary-evidence procedures. Link each item to an RQ. Include non-ideal branches, success/assistance definitions where relevant, and a deviation log.

Do not start fieldwork until unresolved consent, access, or serious validity issues are handled. A draft can remain useful without being marked run-ready.

## Four durable practices

Use the source's mentorship principles as working habits: learn unfamiliar methods rather than cling to a catalogue; use AI to compare methods critically; make decisions defensible; build conversational skill through practice and review. Practice does not remove the need for participant comfort or basic safeguards.
