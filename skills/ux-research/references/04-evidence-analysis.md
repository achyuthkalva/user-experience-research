# 04 — Turn existing material into traceable findings

**Source basis:** Playbook v1.2 §3.1.1, §3.3, §7.3–7.4; Prism 01–03, including H1 and H2. Inventory, coverage states, identifiers, batching, and stricter counting are operational extensions.

## Establish what is available before saying what it proves

Inventory source ID, title/location, type, date, population, collection context, original purpose, permissions, completeness, and status. Use reviewed / partially reviewed / not reviewed / inaccessible / excluded. Document exclusions and limits. A listed recording is not a reviewed recording.

Distinguish direct participant evidence, operational measurements, stakeholder accounts, expert assessment, programme/product intent, and external research. A curriculum document describes intended learning, not necessarily experienced learning. A roadmap records intended product work, not demonstrated impact. Multiple documents repeating one interview are not independent corroboration.

For an existing-evidence audit, map each RQ to the strongest available material and mark coverage sufficient / partial / conflicting / absent, with a reason and remaining uncertainty. “Sufficient” means adequate for this decision and claim, not complete knowledge. Return what research can be removed, reduced, replaced, or deferred, plus what must still be learned and why.

Intentional friction is a claim about design purpose. Keep that separate from evidence about experienced difficulty, effectiveness, unintended consequences, and inclusion. Do not assume all friction is harmful or that stated intent makes it beneficial.

## Step 1 — Confirm the frame before filtering

Restate the decision, research goal, RQs, population, and relevant period. Use H1. Reuse an existing confirmation. Without confirmation, label a reasonable supplied frame assumed and preserve broad evidence until it is reviewed. Do not fabricate a plan from scattered participant quotes.

Maintain two streams: evidence relevant to current RQs, and unexpected/disconfirming/out-of-scope signals with source references. Do not throw away inconvenient evidence because it does not fit the initial hypothesis.

## Step 2 — Organise observations before explaining them

Review sources systematically. For each evidence item record:

- Study/source/participant/session IDs; exact available locator; relevant RQ.
- Evidence type: quotation, paraphrase, reported behaviour, observed behaviour, measured event, or contextual artifact.
- Factual observation, task/context, assistance or contamination, and collection limits.
- Optional tentative interpretation, alternative explanation, and verification need in separate fields.

Use quotation marks only for words checked against the supplied source. Preserve wording; identify translations as translations. A summary must remain a paraphrase. Transcripts usually cannot establish unspoken visual actions or emotion unless these are explicitly recorded. “They were confused” is an interpretation unless the evidence supports that description; document the action or exact statement first.

“I do this weekly” is reported behaviour. An observed failure in a study is observed behaviour in that task context, not proof of every real-world failure. A single anomalous observation can be important; repeated evidence is not the only valid signal.

Do not force an explanation onto each observation. “Interpretation not established” preserves an important boundary.

## Step 3 — Consolidate without erasing differences

Group evidence by RQ and meaningful context. Analyse within relevant participant groups before comparing groups. Keep dates, product versions, source types, and collection methods distinguishable. Maintain a small coding dictionary with code definitions and inclusion/exclusion examples; record important changes and revisit earlier coding when needed.

Identify repeated patterns, exceptions, positive outcomes, disconfirming cases, and under-covered segments. Trace each cluster back to evidence IDs. Distinguish absence of evidence, absence of an event in a measured opportunity, and evidence of an absence.

### Count unique eligible units

Define the unit before calculating. A person, session, task attempt, ticket, account, and organisation are different units.

For participant findings use X unique participants with the documented outcome / N eligible participants with usable evidence for that question or task. Record total recruited/attended separately. For each count preserve the participants included, excluded, unexposed, missing, and assisted as relevant. Do not treat unknown as no, merge identity matches without evidence, or count one participant twice for repeated quotes.

When multiple outcomes can overlap, state that. Do not sum categories into a unique-person total without the overlap information. If N is unknown, report the known count and that the denominator is unavailable. When pooling would mislead, show subgroup counts instead.

With partial task data, report observed X/N plus the number of missing or unexposed participants; do not silently change denominators to make results favourable. Assistance should be an outcome category, not an invisible exclusion. Counts support descriptive sample claims, not unsupported population estimates.

### Handle large inputs in batches

Record the source universe first. Chunk by participant/session or source unit where possible. For every batch log its boundaries, read status, evidence IDs, RQ coverage, new codes, exceptions, and unresolved identity/locator issues. Carry the context record, not a lossy executive summary, into the next batch. Deduplicate globally before counts. Mark the synthesis partial until all required batches are processed.

Do not claim full audio/video review from transcripts, selected clips, or excerpts. Ask for usable text or appropriate access when required. State precisely what was reviewed.

## Step 4 — Separate findings from candidate explanations

For each RQ produce:

**Finding:** a bounded factual statement, count when defensible, context, and supporting evidence IDs.

**Interpretation/insight:** a concise named explanation or implication; evidence linking it to the finding; contrary evidence; plausible alternatives; and limits. Use “suggests” or “may” when warranted, not ungrounded causal certainty.

**Follow-up:** the smallest additional evidence that would distinguish alternatives or reduce a material decision risk. A new hypothesis is not a new validated finding.

Apply H2 to mixed, surprising, or uncertain interpretations. Record human feedback. An approved interpretation is still bounded by the evidence.

Do not assign severity automatically in an analysis-only task. When prioritisation is requested, hand evidence, interpretations, counts, gaps, and frame status to 05. Use T7–T8 in 08.
