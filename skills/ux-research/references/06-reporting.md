# 06 — Communicate the decision without overstating the evidence

**Source basis:** Prism 05–06; the linked McKinsey Framing Playbook; Playbook §3.1.1 and §7.4. The framing title is the name of the owner's resource, not a claim of McKinsey authorship, endorsement, or validation.

## Establish the communication contract

Identify the audience, intended decision, format, time available, and current approval state. Use available information rather than repeatedly asking. Reporting should support understanding, alignment, direction, institutional memory, and considered change. Do not tailor the conclusion to what stakeholders want to hear.

Use source-linked findings and reviewed interpretations. For a report-only task, inspect existing artifacts and flag missing context or limitations instead of inventing the underlying study. A findings-only request does not require recommendations.

## Preserve Prism's five-section report structure

### A — Executive summary

Lead with what the evidence supports. Summarise the study, the most important questions, actual participant/source coverage, key findings/insights, the decision implication, and material limitations. State whether actions are provisional or approved. Aim for a short read; do not force exactly three insights when fewer are supported.

### B — Study overview

Include the decision, research goal, all RQs, method, relevant population/segments, period, actual included sample/source count, inclusion/exclusion, scope, constraints, deviations, evidence limitations, and frame status. Participant descriptions should be relevant and privacy-conscious; names, ages, or employers are not mandatory fields.

### C — Prioritised insights

Use a summary table when it improves navigation. For each insight include the original seven fields: RQ; finding/issue; contextual description; insight; severity and justification; recommendation or pending status; and participant count. Add evidence IDs/locators, counterevidence, claim limits, and approval status. Show highest provisional severity first when appropriate, but distinguish that from an approved delivery order. Positive findings or unrated issues should not be forced into the defect scale.

### D — Additional information

Keep technical/session limitations, out-of-scope opportunities, positive findings, and next research questions visible. Do not use this section to bury evidence that materially weakens a headline claim; material counterevidence belongs beside the claim too.

### E — Dissemination assets

Reference actual approved quotes, clips, screenshots, or other proof points, with source and permission notes. Use assets contextually or in a media index. If unavailable, say so. Do not fabricate clips or quotations to meet the original checklist's asset minimum.

## Apply the framing dependency as a communication profile

Retain the six ideas: answer-first structure; headings that communicate meaning; coherent top-level grouping; conclusion → support → detail → caveats; semantic navigation; and continuity through stable terminology and carried-forward context.

Default to simple headings, short sentences, explicit labels, and compact comparisons. Use exactly three top-level pillars only when that framing is requested or fits without distorting the material. A procedure can have five phases; an evidence set can have two findings. Reporting preferences must never determine the evidence count.

For long narrative documents, make headings tell a coherent story. For reference logs, instruments, and templates, descriptive labels often enable faster retrieval. Use both where useful: “Evidence is partial — Coverage by research question.” Do not make every table or brief carry repeated opening and closing verdicts.

Use text labels for meaning even when colours or emojis are available: Decision / Evidence / Limitation / Approval required. Plain Markdown should remain understandable without Notion toggles, callout colours, or a specific renderer. Colour must not be the only signal.

Keep terminology consistent across phases. Explain RQ and other abbreviations on first use. Put crucial source context and caveats in the section that uses them so readers need not recall earlier pages.

## Match the output to the medium

For a presentation, produce one principal point per slide with enough local context and a visible evidence reference; put detail in supporting notes or an appendix. For a short stakeholder message, state the result, evidence limit, and requested decision. For a workshop, distinguish facts, interpretations, options, and decisions. Do not create files or publish messages unless requested and supported by available tools.

Before delivery, check source accuracy, relevant denominators, protected identities, H3 status, honest uncertainty, and whether the reader knows what remains unresolved. Persuasive communication must not outrun the evidence.
